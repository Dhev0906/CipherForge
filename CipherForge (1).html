Step 2

Seal & sign a message

Encrypting with the recipient's public key gives confidentiality — only their private key can open it. Signing with your own private key gives authentication and non-repudiation — they know it came from you, and you can't later deny sending it.

Signing proves authorship. Skipping it still keeps the message confidential, just not attributable.

Step 3

Open & verify a message

Decrypting proves nothing about who sent a message — only your private key and passphrase are needed for that. Add the sender's public key too, and CipherForge checks whether their signature actually matches, catching tampering or impersonation.
